Closed-form biaxial curvature and ductility domains for reinforced concrete rectangular sections

Sberna A.P., Di Trapani Fabio
Engineering Structures — October 2026

Abstract

The accurate yet practical assessment of deformation capacity of reinforced concrete (RC) members under axial load and biaxial bending is a key challenge in performance-based seismic engineering. This study develops a new set of closed-form analytical expressions for the analytical approximation of the biaxial elastic curvature, ultimate curvature and curvature ductility domains of rectangular RC cross-sections. An extensive parametric dataset of curvature domains is generated, spanning a wide range of geometrical configurations, reinforcement ratios, and axial load levels. The resulting directional capacity data are then approximated using a super-ellipse equation with the shape exponents calibrated via symbolic regression using a multi-population genetic programming (GP) algorithm. The resulting closed-form equations accurately capture the complex direction-dependent nature of the domains, showing superior accuracy and generality when compared to existing formulations. The proposed model successfully balances high fidelity while maintaining computational efficiency of a closed-form solution, ensuring compactness and ease of integration into structural analysis and design tools. The results will show that the model can balance accuracy and usability for performance-based assessment, displacement-based design, and the calibration of lumped-plasticity nonlinear models of RC columns.

Highlights

  • Closed-form expressions are proposed for the biaxial ultimate curvature, elastic curvature and curvature ductility domains of rectangular RC sections under axial load.
  • A fiber-based parametric database was generated from 227 cross-sections. It contains 13,620 ultimate and 6,251 elastic curvature and ductility domains, covering a wide range of geometries, reinforcement layouts and axial load levels.
  • Each domain is fitted with a super-ellipse. Its shape exponent, which controls the transition from concave to convex domain shapes, is derived by symbolic regression with multi-population genetic programming.
  • Axial load level, longitudinal reinforcement ratio and rebar spacing turn out to be the key explanatory variables. The predicted exponents reach R² ≈ 0.94, 0.85 and 0.87 for the ultimate, elastic and ductility domains, respectively.
  • The model is more accurate than existing formulations. Mean errors drop from 7.8% to 1.2% as the axial load ratio increases. This makes the model suitable for displacement-based design and for calibrating lumped-plasticity models of RC columns.
